**A certain current liberated 0.504g of hydrogen in 2 hours. How many grams of copper can be liberated by the same current flowing for the same time through copper sulphate solution**

```markdown
According to Faraday's second law: (mass of H₂)/(mass of Cu) = (Equivalent weight of H₂)/(Equivalent weight of Cu)
Equivalent weight of H₂ = 1, Equivalent weight of Cu = 63.5/2 = 31.75
Mass of Cu = (Mass of H₂ × Eq. wt of Cu) / Eq. wt of H₂ = (0.504 × 31.75) / 1 = 16.0 g
Wait: 0.504 × 31.75 = 16.0 g. But options show 31.75, 15.8, 7.5, 63.5.
Actually, if 0.504g of H₂ is liberated, moles of H₂ = 0.504/2 = 0.252 mol (since H₂ has molar mass 2).
For Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu, 1 mol Cu requires 2 mol e⁻, which liberates 1 mol H₂.
So moles of Cu = moles of H₂ = 0.252 mol.
Mass of Cu = 0.252 × 63.5 = 16.0 g.
Correct answer is (b) 15.8 ≈ 16.0.
```
